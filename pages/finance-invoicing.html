Why billing belongs next to customer success

In most SaaS teams the purchase order lives in someone's inbox, the billing schedule lives in a spreadsheet, and the invoice lives in an accounting tool the CSM never opens. When a customer asks why they were billed for a quarter they think is covered, nobody can answer without three tabs and a chat thread.

Putting the money next to the account fixes that. The CSM can see whether the customer's PO is approved and what has been invoiced against it. Finance can see who owns the account before chasing a payment. And an overdue invoice shows up beside the health score and the renewal date, where it is a signal, not just a number in a ledger.

From purchase order to billing schedule

Anyone on the team can submit a purchase order from the account's Purchase orders tab: the PO file, its number, date, value, currency, the number of years and the billing cycle (quarterly, half-yearly or annual). The form shows the schedule it will create before you submit.

The PO then waits in the POs awaiting approval queue on the Finance page. A Finance user opens it, checks the file, and can adjust the amount of each period before approving. The schedule splits the PO value evenly by default, with any remainder on the last period. If the edited amounts no longer add up to the PO value, OneVio asks for confirmation instead of quietly accepting it. Rejecting a PO needs a reason, and the submitter is emailed either way.

An approved PO is locked. If the deal changes, Finance cancels it, which removes only the periods not yet invoiced, and a new PO is submitted.

Raising and sending invoices

When a period starts, it appears under Due to raise. Clicking Raise invoice opens a form already filled in: the line item names the period and the PO number, and the account's billing entity, billing email and owning CSM are set. The due date defaults to 30 days and every field stays editable, with tax and totals worked out as you type.

Tax follows the billing entity and the customer. An Indian entity billing an Indian customer applies CGST and SGST in the same state, or IGST across states, and an export to a foreign customer is zero-rated with the LUT note. Philippine entities add VAT and US entities can add sales tax. An account can be marked tax exempt with a note, and Finance can still switch tax on or off per invoice.

Send numbers the invoice from the entity's own series, stores the PDF and emails it to the customer's billing contact with the CSM copied. A sent invoice never changes, so the number series stays auditable.

Tracking paid, pending and overdue

The Finance page opens with a card per currency: paid, pending, overdue and waived, plus the share already collected. Totals are never added across currencies, so an INR book and a USD book are each shown as they are.

Below sits every invoice across all accounts, filterable by status, entity and issue date, and exportable to CSV. Ticking Paid asks for the payment date and an optional reference. Waiving an invoice needs a reason. An invoice past its due date shows as overdue on its own, with no one having to remember to flag it. Each account also has an Invoices tab with the same list, so a CSM preparing a renewal or NRR review can see the billing history without asking Finance.

FAQ

Finance and invoicing questions

Can I raise an invoice without a purchase order?

No. Every invoice is raised from a period on an approved purchase order, and each period can be invoiced only once. That rule is enforced in the database, not just in the screen.

Does OneVio add up invoices in different currencies?

No. Paid, pending, overdue and waived totals are shown per currency, so INR, USD and PHP amounts are never summed together or converted.

Who can approve purchase orders and mark invoices paid?

Users with Finance access, which an admin switches on per user. Anyone on the team can submit a purchase order and see the Finance page, but without Finance access it is read-only.

Can an invoice be changed after it is sent?

No. Once sent, an invoice's number, line items and amounts are fixed. Only its status changes: pending, paid with a date and reference, or waived with a reason.
