A renewal playbook, 90 days to signature

A renewal playbook is a fixed set of steps that starts 90 days before the renewal date and ends with a signed contract and working billing. Each step has a date, an owner and a clear finish line. This guide gives you the seven steps, the stages to track and a simple 90-day forecast.

Why 90 days

Renewals rarely fail on the renewal date. They fail weeks earlier, when nobody noticed that usage dropped or that the champion left. By the time the quote goes out, it is too late to fix the product problem behind a "no".

Ninety days gives you room for three things. You can find and fix problems while the customer still has time to see results. You can send a quote early enough for their budget and procurement process. And you can negotiate without a deadline forcing a discount. For large accounts, or accounts with a slow buying process, start even earlier.

The 7 steps

Count each step back from the renewal date. Owners below are a common split. Change them to fit your team, but give every step exactly one owner.

Steps 1 and 2 are about the customer. Steps 3 to 7 are about the paperwork. Teams that skip the first two often get to step 5 and find out the customer was never going to renew.

Match the playbook to health

One playbook for every account is a fine start. It works better when the timing follows the account's health score.

  • Green accounts can follow the default dates. Use the kickoff call to talk about growth.
  • Yellow accounts need the health review done early. Fix the gaps before the quote goes out.
  • Red accounts should start sooner, at 120 days or more. Bring in a senior sponsor and agree a recovery plan before you talk about price.

Do not send a renewal quote to a Red account and hope. Fix the reason it is Red first.

Renewal stages to track

Steps tell you what to do. Stages tell everyone where a renewal stands. Six are enough:

  • Not started: the renewal is coming but no one has spoken to the customer yet.
  • Outreach: the kickoff call or first contact has happened.
  • Quote sent: the customer has a price in hand.
  • Negotiating: price, term or scope is being discussed.
  • Committed: the customer has agreed, even if the contract is not signed yet.
  • At risk: there is a real chance the customer will not renew, or will cut back.

At risk can happen at any point. Move a renewal there as soon as you know, not when you are sure.

A 90-day forecast

With stages in place, a renewal forecast is four numbers for the next 90 days:

  • Due next 90 days: all renewal revenue with a renewal date in the window.
  • Commit: revenue from renewals in the Committed stage.
  • Best case: everything due except renewals marked At risk.
  • At risk: revenue from renewals marked At risk.

Review these every week. If commit is far below best case close to the window, too many renewals are stuck in the middle stages. A count of renewals still Not started inside 90 days is a useful early warning too. To see what the losses do to your retention numbers, try the churn rate calculator and the NRR calculator.

Mistakes that cost renewals

  • Starting at the quote. If the first renewal contact is a price, you have skipped the part where you find problems.
  • No single owner. A step owned by "the team" is a step nobody does.
  • Hiding risk. A renewal left in Negotiating when everyone knows it is shaky makes the forecast wrong.
  • Forgetting billing. A signed contract with no invoice set up still looks like churn a month later.
  • Losing past-due renewals. A renewal that passed its date without a signature must stay visible until it is closed.

FAQ

Renewal playbook questions

When should a renewal process start?

Start 90 days before the renewal date for most annual contracts. That leaves time to fix problems, send a quote, negotiate and get a signature. Start earlier for large or unhealthy accounts, and for customers with a long procurement process.

Who owns the renewal, the CSM or the account manager?

It depends on your team. Many teams have the CSM own the relationship and health review, and the account manager own the quote and negotiation. What matters is that every step has one named owner, so nothing waits for someone else.

What is the difference between commit and best case?

Commit is renewal revenue the customer has agreed to, even if the contract is not signed yet. Best case is everything due except renewals marked at risk. The gap between the two shows how much of the forecast still depends on work you have not finished.

What should I do when a renewal is at risk?

Mark it at risk as soon as you know, so the forecast is honest. Find out the real reason, bring in a senior sponsor on both sides, and agree a short recovery plan with dates. Do not wait for the quote stage to raise it.

How do I handle a renewal that is already past due?

Keep it visible until it is closed. Agree a short extension or a signed date with the customer, check that billing does not lapse, and record why it slipped. Past-due renewals usually point to a step that started too late.
